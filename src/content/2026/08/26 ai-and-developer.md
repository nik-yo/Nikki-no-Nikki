# AI and Developer
::

<br/>

I went to a meetup event and we had interesting discussion on AI which makes me think especially on what will happen to developers onshore and offshore.

<br/>

One of the attendees mentioned about his employer decided not to use more expensive models to save costs especially when they have many offshore workers. I've thought about that before. Considering offshore is cheaper per hour compared to onshore, that means AI cost can take up a bigger percentage of total cost. 

<br/>

Resorting to locally run AI won't be cheap too considering to get reasonable performance, a beefy hardware is required and AI demand has driven the hardware cost to multiple of times higher.

<br/>

And then, another attendee brought up a good point. Few years from now, the current frontier models will be outdated but still capable and will cost a fraction of the latest model. That makes me think. A developer will get a pay raise, so developer will cost more over time. A certain model will cost less over time. That means unless a developer is able to bring more value, they will lose to a model (either latest or older model) per dollar in terms of writing code.

<br/>

Yet another attendee brought up a point where code generated by AI can look good, but might not be architecturally sound and have runtime bugs. Runtime bugs are what I frequently experience when using AI. I can't help but feel developer's responsibility as code writer will be threatened if not already is, unless they move to a different role, for example, architect. Aside from experience, I feel knowledge in both AI and technical architecture will be even more important in the future. No longer it matters on how fast you can code or how good the code will be, but whether a transformed developer can design a reliable architecture and able to steer AI in implementing the design. Knowledge and experience will be the glue between human and AI and both are required.
